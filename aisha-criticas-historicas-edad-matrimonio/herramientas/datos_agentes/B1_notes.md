## Findings
- Vives censure (Tomus II): wife named Aysa/Ayssa; adultery accusation (LUST); "utpote quam uirginem acceperat, & adhuc iuuenculam" (AGE). Probably the earliest explicit youth statement in this series; printed 1543, Vives d. 1540.
- Chronica mendosa (Tomus I): no Aisha found. Corpus Cluniacense gives Robert of Ketton as translator, c. 1142-43; the catalogue's "eodem interprete" is ambiguous.
- Riccoldo: Tomus II Confutatio is Bartholomaeus Piceno's back-translation from Kydones' Greek (catalogue). No Aisha in the Tomus I Riccoldo passages read.
- Melanchthon/Luther Praemonitio: same text, two attributions (Melanchthon in the McGill title and CR 5; "D. Martini Lutheri" in Source Library p. 7). Generic criticism, no Aisha.
- "Aiffa uxor Machumet" (Tomus II codex passage; the first wave's name): probably Hafsa by context, unverified.

## Negative results
- Luther WA 53 pp. 261-396 and 569-572: not read; Aischa, Hafsa and Zainab neither confirmed nor refuted.
- Abu Bakr forms (Abubacer etc.): no hit in the Bibliander or 1500 OCRs.
- Improbatio 1500: no Aisha in the OCR.
